# Model Routing

DesignLab 2.0 spends expensive intelligence only where it materially improves the finished product.

## Scripts / deterministic tools — first choice
Use for:
- repository inventory;
- route/component extraction;
- diffs;
- commit/hash checks;
- token and file-size checks;
- screenshot capture;
- automated tests;
- design-token validation;
- visual-diff measurements.

Do not pay an LLM to do deterministic work.

## Sonnet — default workhorse
Use for:
- Product Truth interpretation;
- Feature Packet creation;
- all four UX persona executions;
- visual-persona reasoning/brief generation;
- prototype specifications;
- routine implementation;
- refactoring;
- routine design and UX QA;
- applying a locked Design Constitution.

Target: roughly 75–85% of AI work after the system stabilises.

## Opus — senior synthesis/escalation
Use for:
- comparing four materially different UX proposals;
- synthesising the user's selected parts into one coherent final UX;
- difficult architectural/product trade-offs;
- final Design Constitution creation;
- difficult cross-system conflicts;
- visual-direction synthesis when judgement is genuinely ambiguous.

Do not use Opus for repetitive production.

## Fable — visual specialist
Use for:
- hero-screen visual exploration;
- visually demanding comparative work;
- selected final visual refinements;
- difficult visual QA where ordinary screenshot comparison is insufficient.

Do not give Fable the repository to discover product truth.

## Escalation test
Escalate only if:
1. the lower-cost route produced a real ambiguity or quality ceiling;
2. the decision is important enough to affect the product broadly;
3. the answer will become a reusable rule or approved design asset.

## Modes
- **Full Tournament:** new product / major redesign.
- **UX Tournament:** genuinely new interaction family.
- **Production Mode:** ordinary screens using locked UX + visual system.
